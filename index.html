Punjab Police App HCI principles mapped to key interface decisions
01
Slide 01

Utility Hub (Home)

Law of Common Region (Gestalt)
Target: Emergency Contacts grouping

By enclosing the three critical emergency numbers (15, 1122, 1787) within a shared, subtly red-bordered container, the interface immediately categorizes them as a distinct, highly related group of lifelines, visually separating them from standard routine services.

Fitts's Law
Target: Massive red SOS button

Fitts's Law dictates that the time required to reach a target is a function of its size and distance. The enormous target area, central placement, and highly distinct red gradient drastically reduce the cognitive and motor precision required to trigger an emergency response under severe panic or duress.

Hick-Hyman Law
Target: 2x3 Quick Services grid

Instead of overwhelming the user by listing dozens of disjointed government features, complex services are abstracted into six clean, top-level categories. This limits choices, prevents choice paralysis, and respects the user's working memory capacity.

Slide 02

Active SOS Dispatch

Aesthetic and Minimalist Design (Signal-to-Noise Ratio)
Target: Pulsing red status indicator

Stripping away all standard navigation, headers, and menus eliminates visual noise. In a high-stress scenario, the interface focuses entirely on a single, massive visual anchor to reassure the user that a signal has been sent and help is being dispatched.

User Control and Freedom
Target: Cancel Request button

Providing a highly visible emergency exit at the bottom of the screen empowers the user to quickly abort accidental triggers. This minimizes user panic regarding false alarms and saves valuable police resources.

03
Slide 03

Full Services Directory

Flexibility and Efficiency of Use
Target: Search services input

While the categorized vertical list aids novice users in browsing, the prominent search bar acts as an accelerator for expert users. It allows them to bypass visual scanning entirely and jump directly to their intended service.

Signifiers and Affordance
Target: Service cards with chevrons

The rounded card container combined with the right-pointing chevron acts as a clear visual signifier. It provides a strong affordance that the entire block is an interactive touch target leading deeper into a specific flow.

04
Slide 04

Complaint - Identity (Step 1)

User Control and Freedom
Target: Guest Access banner

Reassuring citizens upfront that they can report incidents securely without navigating a tedious account creation or login flow removes a critical friction point, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for reporting.

Visibility of System Status
Target: 1-2-3 progress stepper

A highly visual timeline sets immediate expectations regarding the length of the form. By keeping the user informed of their exact position (Identity - Incident - Evidence), the design reduces fatigue and minimizes form abandonment rates.

05
Slide 05

Complaint - Incident (Step 2)

Match Between System and the Real World
Target: Plain-language form labels

The interface avoids intimidating technical police jargon (e.g., Penal Code, Jurisdiction Area). Formulating inputs in natural language aligns the system perfectly with the citizen's everyday mental model of explaining an event.

Error Prevention
Target: Next action button

Explicitly labeling the button Next rather than Submit on the second step provides clear system feedback that the process is incomplete. This prevents the user from prematurely finalizing the report before they have a chance to attach vital evidence.

06
Slide 06

Complaint - Evidence (Step 3)

Affordance
Target: Dashed upload dropzone

The dashed border and central upward-arrow icon provide a universally recognized visual affordance. It intuitively communicates that the area is interactive and ready to receive file uploads, without requiring dense instructional text.

Error Prevention (Constraints)
Target: Legal declaration checkbox

Forcing the user to actively check a box to confirm the truthfulness of their report introduces a hard constraint. This physical interaction protects the system from spam and prevents accidental, blind submissions.

07
Slide 07

Success Confirmation

Visibility of System Status
Target: Massive green checkmark

The interface provides immediate, unambiguous, and reassuring visual feedback that the system has successfully processed a high-stakes task, instantly eliminating user anxiety regarding whether their report went through.

Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover
Target: Tracking ID card and Track Status

Proactively providing the Tracking ID and a direct button to track it anticipates the user's next logical task. It offers immediate utility, ensuring the user does not have to hunt for a tracking page later.

Slide 08

Timeline Tracker

Match Between System and the Real World
Target: Vertical timeline visualization

The vertical timeline structure maps perfectly to a human's understanding of chronological progression. Differentiating completed steps (green checkmarks) from active (blue highlight) and pending (grey outlines) makes the investigation status instantly scannable.

Slide 09

PKM Locator Map

Progressive Disclosure (Aesthetic and Minimalist Design)
Target: Floating bottom details sheet

Instead of overwhelming the user with a dense, text-heavy list of addresses, the interface progressively reveals detailed information (distance, operating hours, directions button) in a bottom sheet only when a specific map pin is selected by the user.

Slide 10

Tenant Registration

Trust and Transparency (Visibility of System Status)
Target: Data verification consent banner

Placing a mandatory consent checkbox at the very top of the screen explicitly informs the user about how their data will be used, establishing institutional trust before they input sensitive personal information.

Law of Proximity (Gestalt)
Target: Landlord and tenant card separation

Breaking a long, intimidating form into visually separated, distinctly titled white cards groups related data together. This spatial separation prevents cognitive overload and reduces data entry errors by the user.

Slide 11

Certificate Request - CNIC Upload

Affordance and Signifiers
Target: Dashed upload dropzones

The dashed borders and central cloud icons act as strong visual signifiers, clearly indicating that these specific areas are interactive zones afforded for clicking to initiate a file upload.

Error Prevention (Constraints)
Target: Explicit file constraints text

By clearly stating the acceptable file formats and maximum size limit upfront, the interface introduces physical system constraints. This informs the user before they act, significantly reducing the likelihood of system errors during upload.

12
Slide 12

Upload Failed Error Modal

Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors
Target: Descriptive error message

The system goes beyond merely stating an error occurred; it precisely diagnoses the problem (file too large) and provides a clear, actionable recovery step (compress the image), adhering to Nielsen's heuristics.

Aesthetic and Minimalist Design (Signal-to-Noise Ratio)
Target: Dimmed background overlay

By darkening the rest of the interface, the system deliberately restricts visibility to eliminate background distractions. This forces the user's cognitive focus entirely onto acknowledging and resolving the critical error state.

13
Slide 13

Arms License - New Application

Consistency and Standards
Target: Segmented control tabs

Utilizing standard top-anchored segmented tabs allows users to effortlessly switch between primary tasks using established mobile UI patterns, ensuring they do not have to learn a novel navigation structure.

Law of Proximity (Gestalt)
Target: Distinct form sections

Grouping highly related input fields into distinct, well-labeled bounding boxes chunks the information. This visual separation drastically reduces cognitive load compared to presenting a single, continuous wall of inputs.

14
Slide 14

Arms License - Renewal

Recognition Rather Than Recall
Target: Pre-filled license details

Automatically populating known data fields relies on the user's ability to simply recognize and verify the accuracy of the information, rather than forcing them to mentally recall and manually type lengthy numerical strings.

Visibility of System Status
Target: Processing fee info box

Displaying the financial requirement conspicuously right above the primary action button ensures the user is fully aware of the system's payment expectations before they commit to proceeding, preventing hidden surprises.

Slide 15

Character Certificate - Application

Visibility of System Status (Expectation Management)
Target: Informational banner

Providing upfront transparency about the physical real-world processing time manages user expectations immediately. This prevents frustration and unnecessary follow-up inquiries regarding the status of the request.

Error Prevention
Target: Purpose of Certificate dropdown

Utilizing a dropdown menu instead of a free-text entry field constrains the user to valid, system-recognized options (e.g., Immigration / Visa). This standardizes the data input and completely eliminates typos or ambiguous entries.

Slide 16

Employee Registration

User Control and Freedom (Informed Consent)
Target: Explicit consent checkbox

Placing a mandatory verification consent declaration at the very entry point of the form ensures ethical design. It empowers the user with full control and understanding of how the inputted data will be utilized before they commit any personal details.

Law of Common Region (Gestalt)
Target: Employer and employee cards

Enclosing the respective sets of data fields within their own distinct, rounded white cards establishes a clear semantic boundary. This helps the user naturally separate their own information from their domestic worker's information, minimizing accidental data entry errors.
